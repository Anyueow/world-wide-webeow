Hi, my name is Ananya! Over the last year and a half, I’ve been thinking a lot about what stands out in an increasingly agentic world. What skills become more valuable? How do I become truly AI-native? And, maybe more importantly, what kind of work do I actually enjoy?

I’ve spent that time in strategy consulting, largely with my head down in data, analyzing it, finding the story in it, and packaging those insights for executives making decisions around million-dollar initiatives. The work has been intellectually challenging, but somewhere along the way I realized that the part I found most interesting wasn’t necessarily the analysis itself. It was figuring out how we could work better.

I became the person constantly looking for ways to help my team move faster: fixing broken processes, automating repetitive work, and making the knowledge we already had easier to access and use. Eventually, I built a knowledge infrastructure system specifically for our brand of knowledge work that helped a 10-person team dramatically accelerate delivery and has since been used to onboard five new people in three months.

That experience clarified something for me: I really like finding problems and figuring out how people and technology can come together to solve them.

In hindsight, that instinct has been there for a long time. I studied Data Science and Finance at Northeastern, but I spent much of college building things and surrounding myself with other people who wanted to build. I competed in the Husky Startup Challenge and later became its director, participated in several accelerators on campus, and co-founded Rev, a community for builders. During my junior year, I also founded a company of my own and spent a year and a half building it, eventually leading an engineering team and almost raising a seed round.

Now, I’m ready to take what I’ve learned in consulting and get much closer to building again.

I want to operate at the intersection of people and technology. That could mean helping scale a fast-growing startup in a generalist go-to-market role, or sitting at the center of a scrappy product team, prototyping quickly and shipping alongside customers.

What I’m looking for now is a company early enough that go-to-market is still a design problem rather than a playbook to execute, somewhere I can sit between product and customer and be accountable to both. I want real ownership rather than a small slice of someone else’s roadmap. And, most importantly, I want to work alongside a small group of people who are exceptionally good at what they do, so that I’m constantly being pushed to get better at what I do.

2018 to now
